EXPLORATION — how deep a fall has to be before it stops bouncing
ewmac32_128, 3095 days, 1141540 symbol-days (227047 of them held by the book)
Depth = fall from the 120-day high, divided by what the name's own volatility says
a move of that many days should be. Nothing is traded and no costs are charged:
this measures whether a deep fall PREDICTS anything, not whether acting on it pays.

'vs same' is the book's names minus EVERY liquid name at the same depth on the same
day, so the market's own move that week cancels. 'regains high' is the share that
got back to the high they fell from within 60 trading days.

Read the SHAPE, not any one row. A smooth decline means there is no level to exit
at; a sign flip that stays flipped is a level.

TWO CAVEATS, both biting hardest in exactly the deep buckets that matter:
  * SURVIVORSHIP. A name needs 60 more bars of history to contribute a forward
    return, so one that falls hard and then stops trading is silently absent. The
    worst outcomes are missing from the deep rows, which flatters them.
  * CLUSTERING. Deep falls happen in crises, so the thousands of observations in
    the tail rows come from a handful of weeks and are nothing like independent.
