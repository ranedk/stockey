# How Indian practitioners trade quarterly results as a buy signal (small/micro/SME focus)

Scope note: about 17 searches and fetches, public pages only. Overall finding: the Indian "results play" material online is mostly **rules without track records**. No source gives an audited or even self-reported multi-year record for an India results-trading method. Everything below is a claim unless it is marked as evidence. The search engine also returned many scraped spam copies on unrelated university/government domains; those were ignored.

## 1. Which results count (thresholds, acceleration, turnaround, Code 33)

### Takeaway
Indian practitioners mostly borrow US definitions: Minervini's "Code 33" (three quarters of accelerating EPS, sales and margins), Stockbee/Bonde's sales growth above ~39% plus a "massive" profit jump, and a plain "EPS growth 25-50%+ with surprises" SEPA checklist. The practitioner versions differ from a single "surprise" quarter in two ways: they require **acceleration over consecutive quarters**, and they put **sales** growth ahead of profit.

### Cited Findings
- Code 33 as coded by Indian Screener.in users is three quarters of acceleration in earnings, sales and margins. One public query reads: "EPS latest quarter > EPS preceding quarter AND EPS preceding quarter > EPS preceding year quarter AND Sales latest quarter > Sales preceding quarter AND Sales preceding quarter > Sales 2quarters back AND Net Profit latest quarter > Net Profit preceding quarter AND Net Profit preceding quarter > Net profit 2quarters back AND EPS latest quarter > 2 ..." — [Screener.in "Code 33 Superperformance Recipe"](https://www.screener.in/screens/102556/code-33-superperformance-recipe/). Several other copies exist: [1](https://www.screener.in/screens/1112478/mark-minervini-code-33), [2](https://screener.in/screens/602587/code-33-mark-minervini), [3](https://screener.in/screens/766317/code33-hitting-all-cylinders-mark-minervini). Note that this query mixes QoQ and YoY comparisons, so it is not a faithful YoY acceleration test.
- MarketInOut runs an India "earnings and sales acceleration" screen (EPS and revenue only, no margins) — [MarketInOut](https://www.marketinout.com/stock-screener/earnings-and-sales-acceleration/india).
- An Indian X account's SEPA checklist: "EPS growth 25–50%+ - Consistent quarterly growth - Earnings surprises preferred. Weak earnings = weak upward drift", followed by a strong-sales-growth step — [Gaurav Srivastava on X](https://x.com/Gaurav_Cx10/status/2030727521596060120).
- Stockbee (Pradeep Bonde) EP criteria (US origin; Indian traders copy them):
  - Sales growth above 39% on consecutive releases signals a high probability of a move up, and "EPS matter less than sales growth".
  - "MA" in MAGNA53 means profit growth of double or triple digits, or a massive beat.
  - Sales growth must be accelerating, because cost cuts are not sustainable growth.
  - Source: [Retail Traders Repository summary of Bonde](https://retailtradersrepository.substack.com/p/pradeep-bonde-episodic-pivots).
- Indian EP thread "Episodic Pivot – Indian Edition" (@DSS_Rajput007, Jul 2025) gives no numeric thresholds. It cites "robust revenue and earnings growth, steady flow of new orders, and consistent quarter-on-quarter improvement" as the common traits of multi-fold rallies — [threadreader](https://threadreaderapp.com/thread/1946493257338282453). Order inflow is named alongside results, which is relevant to our order-wins work.
- Vijay Kedia's SMILE is a business-quality framework, not a results trigger: Small in size, Medium in experience, Large in aspiration, eXtra-large market potential — [Trendlyne portfolio X-ray](https://trendlyne.com/posts/3912672/portfolio-x-ray-vijay-kedias-smile-is-his-investing-secret). News coverage of his holdings after results is anecdotal, with winners and losers: one holding fell about 10% after weak Q3 results — [DSIJ](https://www.dsij.in/dsijarticledetail/tag/siyaram-silk-mills-ltd); another rose 13% after Q2 FY26 — [Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/markets/stocks/story/this-vijay-kedia-backed-stock-zoomed-over-13-after-q2-results-do-you-own-500155-2025-10-30).
- Basant Maheshwari's earnings rule is long-horizon, not quarterly:
  - "If earnings keep going up, and potential is still not fully utilized, one must not feel shy about buying stocks, even at higher levels" — [CFA Society India event summary](https://cfasocietyindia.org/book-reading-event-the-thoughtful-investor/).
  - Judge on earnings "only if they have a three-year view" — [BloombergQuint](https://www.bloombergquint.com/amp/bq-blue-exclusive/alpha-moguls-markets-wont-top-off-anytime-soon-says-basant-maheshwari).
  - A secondary summary attributes to him a rule of 25-30% sales growth for 5-6 years — [Value Research](https://www.valueresearchonline.com/stories/27492/finding-multi-baggers-the-basant-maheshwari-way/).
- An expectations caveat used by Indian educators: a company reporting 18% profit growth against 20% expected can fall, because the market prices in expectations — [TradingView "Quarterly Results Trading", TechnicalExpress, Aug 2025](https://my.tradingview.com/chart/IN10/GMS44y5O-Quarterly-Results-Trading). Equitymaster makes the same point: a good quarter does not automatically mean a higher price — [Equitymaster](https://www.equitymaster.com/timeless-reading/q1-results-latest-news-and-updates).

### Inferences
- Our test used a single-quarter "surprise". Practitioners require multi-quarter acceleration (Code 33), sales-led growth (Bonde's 39%), or a growth path the market has not priced in. A follow-up test could add "consecutive accelerating quarters" and "sales growth ≥ ~40% for two quarters" as separate, pre-registered variants.
- "Best ever quarter" and "loss to profit" turnaround as explicit rules: I found no Indian source stating them with thresholds (see Gaps).

### Gaps
- No Indian source found with explicit thresholds for "best ever quarter" sales/profit or for loss-to-profit turnarounds.
- No source found on concall guidance as a coded rule.
- The Gaurav Srivastava X post was seen only as a search snippet and was not fetched in full.
- Mukul Agrawal's results approach was not researched (no time left in the budget).

## 2. Timing: before results, results day, gap, delayed entry, holding period

### Takeaway
The Indian EP school splits the reaction into types. "Break & Go" means buying the gap with intraday-high entries, and the Indian thread itself says its accuracy is lower in India. The two delayed types, "Burst & Base" and "Shake & Base", wait for a new base to form after results and buy its breakout. Generic Indian guides say to wait for the first session's reaction and to skip small or choppy reactions. Buying before results is described as very high risk, and no source supports it with data.

### Cited Findings
- From the Indian EP thread [@DSS_Rajput007](https://threadreaderapp.com/thread/1946493257338282453):
  - If the stock gaps up above the contraction zone, enter on a break of the 1-, 3- or 15-minute high of the day. With no gap, the base high is the pivot.
  - Break & Go accuracy "tends to be lower in Indian markets", and follow-through is often weak. The author blames circuit limits restricting "immediate large-scale institutional participation".
  - Burst & Base: the stock surges on the reaction day, then forms a flag or tight base over "the following weeks", which gives a cleaner entry.
  - Shake & Base: the reaction day draws no fresh buying, and the opportunity is the later breakout from the new base.
  - Stocks that ran up before results can show a muted reaction.
  - The thread advertises a paid Telegram channel, so the author has a commercial interest.
- Bonde (US): enter normally at the open, or wait a few minutes, or use a next-day "Delayed Reaction" entry. He sets a rough profit target by catalyst strength and "won't rush to exit a 30% gain if he expects a 60–70% move" — [RTR](https://retailtradersrepository.substack.com/p/pradeep-bonde-episodic-pivots).
- Indian generic guide (TradingView, TechnicalExpress) describes four phases:
  - pre-results positioning, rated "very high risk";
  - results-day intraday trading;
  - stabilisation over 1–3 days;
  - post-results trends that "can persist for about 1–4 weeks".
  - Its lower-risk version is going long for a few weeks after strong results, positive commentary and high-volume buying. It gives no data — [TradingView](https://my.tradingview.com/chart/IN10/GMS44y5O-Quarterly-Results-Trading).
- OneTradeJournal (India guide):
  - "Wait for the result and the first session reaction before committing capital."
  - "Skip the trade entirely when the reaction is small or choppy, as drift is unreliable there."
  - Most results are declared after market hours, so the tradable move is the next open.
  - It concedes "Past earnings reactions do not repeat reliably." No statistics are given.
  - Source: [OneTradeJournal](https://onetradejournal.com/learn/earnings-season-trading).
- A Nifty earnings-gap blog (April 2026, unverified, no method shown):
  - skips gaps above 8% at the open, with a stop set at giving back the full gap;
  - claims a pullback entry wins more often (71%) but earns less per winner (+5.1% vs +7.8%) than buying the gap.
  - Source: [tradingzenith](https://tradingzenith-net.pages.dev/artigos/earnings-momentum-trading-nifty). Treat as anecdote.
- Minervini/SEPA holding period, as Indian explainers repeat it: a few weeks to several months, entering on the pivot breakout on expanding volume with the stop below the last contraction — [financialtechwiz](https://www.financialtechwiz.com/post/mark-minervini-trading-strategy/).

### Inferences
- The main difference from our test: we entered at the close after publication on every surprise. Practitioners (a) filter by how the price reacts, skipping small or choppy reactions and wanting gap-and-hold on high volume, and/or (b) wait weeks for a post-results base and buy its breakout. Option (b) changes both entry date and price and adds a second, price-based condition. Our 60-day vs-industry window would also mix their winners (trend held) with trades they would have stopped out early.
- The Indian thread's admission that gap-buying follow-through is weak in India is consistent with our failed 2023-24 check. It argues for a delayed-entry variant rather than result-day entry.

### Gaps
- No Indian quantitative evidence found on pre-results run-ups (buying before the board-meeting date).
- No India study found comparing gap-day entry with delayed base-breakout entry after results.

## 3. Stock filters (market cap, SME, float, promoter, Stage 2, RS, volume)

### Takeaway
Filters that practitioners state:
- Stage 2 / Minervini trend template (price above the 50/150/200-day averages and near highs);
- neglect before the catalyst;
- high volume on the reaction day;
- small size (Bonde: $10–100M, which is roughly Rs 80–800 cr).

No Indian source states explicit promoter-holding, float or SME-specific results rules.

### Cited Findings
- Bonde's filters:
  - neglected for 2 months to 1–3 years, with little price action, news, fund ownership or analyst coverage;
  - market cap below $10M or, more usually, $10–100M;
  - IPO within 10 years;
  - "9 Million EP": volume of 9M+ on a previously low-volume stock.
  - Source: [RTR](https://retailtradersrepository.substack.com/p/pradeep-bonde-episodic-pivots).
- Indian EP thread: Break & Go reactions come with "exceptionally high volumes", with no numeric multiple given — [threadreader](https://threadreaderapp.com/thread/1946493257338282453).
- Minervini Stage 2 template as an Indian Screener.in screen: price against the 50/150/200-day averages and near highs — [Screener.in Stage 2 + VCP](https://www.screener.in/screens/2808965/stage-2-vcp-setup-minervini-style/). The trend template is pass/fail on all 8 criteria — [snappchart](https://www.snappchart.app/blog/beginner-playbook/mark-minervini-trading-strategy).
- SME context: upper circuits in SME stocks "may also reflect thin trading volumes typical of SME stocks" (this point came from an unreliable scraped page; it is consistent with common knowledge). The Nifty SME Emerge index is built from firms with post-issue paid-up capital up to Rs 25 crore — [Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/amp/story/news/forget-ril-and-adani-these-sme-stocks-gave-upto-1-700-return-in-2022-outperforming-nifty-50-index-news-250578). SME firms report half-yearly, not quarterly (background knowledge, not sourced here).

### Inferences
- Bonde's size band ($10–100M) and his neglect requirement point below our Rs 5 cr/day liquidity floor. Practitioners' best cases may sit in stocks we exclude. Any test of that band must charge realistic impact costs and account for circuit-limited fills.
- Requiring neglect (low prior volume, flat price) is the opposite of buying already-extended Stage 2 leaders. The two schools filter differently, and it may be worth testing them as separate pre-registered arms.

### Gaps
- No Indian source found with promoter-holding or low-float rules tied to results trades.
- No Indian source found with a coded SME/Emerge half-yearly-results strategy.

## 4. Exits and stops, including result-day gaps that fade

### Takeaway
Stops are tight and price-based:
- Bonde: about 2.5%, or 10% when he expects a triple-digit move.
- The Indian EP thread: low of day, half of the 14-period ATR, or a VWAP break.
- Indian generic guides: below the reaction-session low.

A fading gap is handled by exiting quickly ("if price hesitates... close the trade") or by waiting for a re-base (Shake & Base).

### Cited Findings
- Bonde normally uses a 2.5% stop, may re-enter after being stopped out if still convinced, and accepts 10% when he expects a triple-digit move. If price hesitates after entry, he sometimes closes, since strong pressure near the open is expected — [RTR](https://retailtradersrepository.substack.com/p/pradeep-bonde-episodic-pivots).
- Indian EP thread: stops at the low of day, half the 14-period ATR or a percentage of ADR, or a VWAP break for intraday. "Initiate with a tight stop and look to carry the position if the stock closes strong" — [threadreader](https://threadreaderapp.com/thread/1946493257338282453).
- Stop "below the session low for longs". "Ordinary stop-losses do not protect you on earnings because the damage happens overnight in a gap." Risk 1–2% of capital per earnings event — [OneTradeJournal](https://onetradejournal.com/learn/earnings-season-trading).

### Inferences
- Practitioner returns come from cutting losers within days and riding the few that trend. A fixed 60-day hold measures something different: the average post-results drift. A stop-and-trail exit variant would test the practitioner claim more fairly, though it adds parameters to the search.

### Gaps
- No source found giving win rates or payoff ratios for these stop rules on Indian data.

## 5. Named practitioners and methods in India; performance evidence

### Takeaway
Visible methods:
- Stockbee EP, translated to India by Twitter/Telegram educators;
- Minervini SEPA / Code 33, as Screener.in community screens;
- Kedia SMILE and Maheshwari's earnings-growth approach, both long-horizon investing styles rather than results-day trades.

No track records were found. The one Indian EP educator found admits Break & Go works less well in India, and sells a paid channel.

### Cited Findings
- @DSS_Rajput007, "Episodic Pivot – Indian Edition": no quantified performance, an admission of lower accuracy in India, and a promoted paid Telegram channel — [threadreader](https://threadreaderapp.com/thread/1946493257338282453).
- TradingView India hosts EP ideas from Indian users (e.g. a gap-up on strong earnings in Supriya Lifesciences). Outcomes are not tallied — [TradingView EP ideas](https://in.tradingview.com/ideas/episodicpivot/).
- Investing.com India runs AI-pick articles highlighting holdings that rose after results ("A 13x profit jump sent this Indian stock up 10%"). These are selective marketing and not EP-specific — [Investing.com](https://in.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/a-13x-profit-jump-sent-this-indian-stock-up-10--our-ai-strategy-was-holding-it-5673485).
- A ValuePickr "Smallcap momentum portfolio" thread is price momentum, not results. Its author says "No back testing done", and posters note leaders "were not maintaining the momentum" — [ValuePickr](https://forum.valuepickr.com/t/smallcap-momentum-portfolio/124410).

### Inferences
- Survivorship is severe. Public examples are winners (Kedia-holding articles, AI-pick articles, chart ideas). No source publishes all signals taken.

### Gaps

- No India-adapted EP course with audited results found.

## 6. Tracking: screens, smallcases, academic evidence

### Takeaway
Results screens (Screener.in Code 33, MarketInOut acceleration, Trendlyne) are widely used but publish no forward returns. I found no smallcase built specifically on results surprises. The academic evidence on Indian post-results drift is mixed and mostly older than our test window:
- earnings momentum was strong in BSE 500 data for 2002-10;
- one NSE study of 2014-18 finds the market efficient;
- one finds drift after bad news only.

### Cited Findings
- Sehgal & Jain (2015), 493 BSE 500 firms, 2002–2010:
  - "Earnings momentum is able to subsume price and revenue momentum."
  - Earnings momentum is the most profitable long-short strategy.
  - A triple-sorted price + earnings + revenue portfolio earned 2.28%/month.
  - Profits persist for up to about 6 months, and are stronger in market upturns.
  - CAPM and Fama-French do not explain the returns.
  - Source: [RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/usm/journl/aamjaf01101_47-84.html).
- Gupta & Dhusia (2021), 100 NSE firms, 2014–2018: the Indian market "tends to be efficient with respect to earnings announcements" and does not produce excess returns — [Trends Economics and Management](https://journals.vutbr.cz/index.php/trends/article/view/541).
- Agarwal & Sridharan (IIMB working paper, 2013): NSE shares over 120 days show "postearnings drift" — [IIMB repository](https://repository.iimb.ac.in/handle/2074/17935).
- Nottingham dissertation, 665 BSE 500 announcements, 2007–2012: efficient after good news; negative drift for over a week after bad news — [Nottingham eprints](https://eprints.nottingham.ac.uk/26569).
- Business Standard (Dec 2017): 34 BSE Smallcap stocks rallied more than 50% (up to 225%) after Sep-2017-quarter results. This is a winners-only list from a smallcap bull year. Seen as a search snippet only; the fetch was blocked — [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/article/markets/34-stocks-from-smallcap-index-rally-over-50-post-september-quarter-results-117122700264_1.html).
- 2026 context: an India TV report says Q2 FY26 results were good, yet midcaps and smallcaps kept falling in October 2026 (Smallcap 250 down 8% from its Sep 8 high) — [India TV](https://www.indiatvnews.com/business/news/why-midcaps-and-smallcaps-are-slumping-despite-nifty-recovery-expert-explains-2026-11-04-1058674). Example of good results not lifting prices when the broader market is falling.
- Chartsmaze offers a "Gap Up backtest" tool for Indian stocks against the Nifty. Its method is not visible publicly — [Chartsmaze](https://chartsmaze.com/gap-up/gap-up-backtest-data).

### Inferences
- The academic record fits our result: earnings momentum and drift were strong in older samples (2002–2010), and evidence weakens in later NSE samples (2014–18 "efficient"). A pass in 2018-22 and a fail in 2023-24 fits a decaying, crowded, regime-dependent effect. Sehgal & Jain's "stronger in upturns" and the 2026 India TV example both point to market regime as a factor.
- Sehgal & Jain combine earnings with price and revenue momentum. The practitioner equivalent is "results + Stage 2/RS + sales growth", which suggests testing a results signal only when the stock also passes a price-trend filter, rather than on all surprises.

### Gaps
- No smallcase with a results-surprise mandate and published returns was found.
- No published forward performance found for Trendlyne/Screener/Tijori results screens; their pages were not fetched.
- The Business Standard 2017 article returned 403, so its details could not be checked.
