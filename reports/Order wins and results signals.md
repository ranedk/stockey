# Others buy different moments in different stocks

The people who say order wins and good results "make money" are mostly not describing the trade we tested. Their gains come from four places that our test design removes on purpose. First, they buy **before or at the very first moment** of the news: on the "lowest bidder" (L1) stage, at the open, or into an upper circuit (the day's price ceiling, where only buyers are left). Second, they **ride a whole sector's rise**, as defence, rail and EPC (engineering-and-construction contractors) did in 2021-24, and we measure against the industry, which cancels that out. Third, they **report only winners**. Fourth, much of the loud SME talk concerns tiny, thin stocks where the jump often cannot be traded and is sometimes manufactured. Nobody we found publishes a full record of every signal taken. The best outside evidence supports our results rather than the claims. The largest study of contract announcements (16,500 US defence awards) finds the average award moves the stock by almost nothing. Only awards that are huge compared with the company's market value add about 0.3% over three days, before costs, which is less than our 0.5% trading cost. The Indian results effect was real in older data (about 4-6% over three months, 2002-2017) and has been shrinking, which fits our pass in 2018-22 and fail in 2023-24. Several rules practitioners actually use differ from ours and can be tested on data we already hold. The 2023-2025 order announcements nobody has read yet are a clean final check for whichever rule is chosen.

## Their gains sit at moments our test never buys

Our test enters at the close of the day after the announcement and holds 60 trading days. The behaviour practitioners describe falls on either side of that window. The fast version happens **before or at the announcement**: stocks often move when the company is declared L1, before the contract is actually awarded. Ashoka Buildcon rose 7.7% on an L1 notice and RVNL moved repeatedly the same way ([DSIJ](https://insights.dsij.in/dsijarticledetail/ashoka-buildcon-emerges-as-l1-bidder-for-2-nhai-projects-in-bihar-stock-jumps-77-per-cent-14770); [Angel One](https://www.angelone.in/news/stocks/rvnl-share-price-surges-on-emerging-as-l1-bidder-for-221-33-crore-railway-epc-project)). In small stocks the jump is often an opening gap straight into the upper circuit, where a buy order may not even fill ([EquitiesIndia](https://equitiesindia.com/glossary/upper-circuit)). Studies abroad show the same shape. Korean contract filings show prices rising **before** the filing and falling back the next day, and the authors raise insider-trading concerns ([DBpia](https://www.dbpia.co.kr/journal/articleDetail?nodeId=NODE10777034)). The US defence study finds the return does not grow from day 3 to day 5, so there is no slow climb to catch afterwards ([TenderAlpha/HKU](https://www.tenderalpha.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/White-Paper-Defense-Contract-Announcements-TenderAlpha-HKU-1.pdf)). In India, insiders trade heavily around results ([NSE-IGIDR](https://nsearchives.nseindia.com/research/content/NSE-IGIDR-WP4-14-15.pdf)). When insiders bought before results, the later slow climb in price disappeared entirely ([Brochet, Lee & Srinivasan](https://www.stern.nyu.edu/sites/default/files/assets/documents/Brochet%20Lee%20Srinivasan%202017%20-%20The%20Role%20of%20Insider%20Trading%20in%20the%20Market%20Reaction%20to%20Earnings%20News.pdf)). An order win is the kind of news that customers, rival bidders and staff know about before it is filed. By the close of the next day, most of whatever was going to happen has probably already happened.

The slow version is **holding a sector for quarters or years**. The cited successes are HAL, BEL, BDL, RVNL, Patel Engineering and Elecon, all from the 2021-24 capex and PSU (government-owned company) run. RVNL rose 125% from March 2023 as brokers pointed to order books 3-4 times yearly revenue ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/markets/news/cpse-index-nears-record-high-after-six-years-rvnl-zooms-125-since-march-123050300312_1.html)). When the run ended, analysts blamed earnings and prices that had run ahead of the business ([Benzinga India](https://in.benzinga.com/content/40363754/party-over-for-defence-and-railway-stocks-analysts-explain-why-hal-bdl-rvnl-ircon-and-other-psu-stoc)). In those stories the single order is incidental. The money came from the whole sector rising, and our industry comparison subtracts exactly that. Even Zerodha's own write-up concludes that announcements "do not tell us whether a stock should go up or down". L&T fell almost 6% on a Rs 15,000 crore order in the same fortnight that Welspun Corp rose on one ([Zerodha Varsity](https://zerodhavarsity.substack.com/p/how-stock-prices-react-to-corporate)).

Results traders also do something we did not do: **they let the price decide**. The Indian guides say to wait for the first session's reaction and skip the trade if the reaction is small or choppy ([OneTradeJournal](https://onetradejournal.com/learn/earnings-season-trading)). The Indian "episodic pivot" school (a big jump on a news catalyst) prefers to wait weeks for the stock to settle into a tight range after results, then buy when it breaks above that range. Its own author admits that buying the gap on results day "tends to be lower" accuracy in India ([threadreader](https://threadreaderapp.com/thread/1946493257338282453)). They also do not hold a fixed 60 days. Stops are tight, around 2.5% or below the reaction day's low, and they keep only the few that keep rising ([Retail Traders Repository on Bonde](https://retailtradersrepository.substack.com/p/pradeep-bonde-episodic-pivots)). Their results are therefore made of many small losses and a few big winners. Our 60-day average blends the trades they would have cut early with the ones they would have kept.

Finally, they want **more than one good quarter**. The widely copied "Code 33" screens on Screener.in need three quarters in a row of rising profit, sales and margins ([Screener.in](https://www.screener.in/screens/102556/code-33-superperformance-recipe/)). Stockbee's rule puts sales growth above about 39% on consecutive reports ahead of profit, because cost cuts are not lasting growth ([RTR](https://retailtradersrepository.substack.com/p/pradeep-bonde-episodic-pivots)). Our surprise test used one quarter.

## The research backs our results, not the claims

**No serious Indian study of order-win announcements exists.** The only India-specific paper covers 15 infrastructure firms after COVID and could not be checked beyond its summary ([RR Journals](https://rrjournals.com/index.php/rrijm/article/view/1823)). Our 2018-22 test is probably the largest one done on this question in India. Abroad, the US defence study is the strongest evidence. Across 16,529 awards, the average three-day move was +0.006% for the average dollar invested and +0.04% for the average stock, both indistinguishable from zero. New awards carried almost no news. Only the top 5% of awards by size against market value earned about +0.3% over three days, before costs, and the authors note that big awards are "partially anticipated" ([TenderAlpha/HKU](https://www.tenderalpha.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/White-Paper-Defense-Contract-Announcements-TenderAlpha-HKU-1.pdf)). A Chinese study finds that government purchase wins can even be **bad news** for highly valued suppliers ([SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=7573758)). That fits our finding that the largest orders (50%+ of revenue) did worst, and the explanation offered by Indian practitioners. Very large orders at contractors strain cash: receivables pile up, government payers are slow, and the profit margin on the work matters more than its size ([DSIJ](https://www.dsij.in/blog/market-blogs-4/order-books-vs-cash-flows-the-real-test-for-infra-epc-companies-233); [Arihant on Vindhya Telelinks](https://arihantresearch.arihantcapital.com/mumbai_research/Vindhya_Telelinks_onepager.pdf)). That explanation is plausible, but we have not tested it.

Results are a different story, because the effect was genuinely there once. On the Nifty 500 from 2002 to 2017, stocks with the best surprises beat those with the worst by **about 4.8% over the following three months**, mostly from the winners rising rather than the losers falling. The gap was already smaller in 2008-17 (4.2%) than in 2002-08 (5.7%), and that study charged no trading costs ([SCIRP](https://file.scirp.org/Html/20-1501629_88060.htm)). A study of 100 NSE firms in 2014-18 found no effect at all ([Trends Economics and Management](https://journals.vutbr.cz/index.php/trends/article/view/541)). A careful US study of 2023-26 data shows the same effect falling from 2.6 points to 0.5 points, and the small-company version going negative in 2025-26 ([guptagaurav.info](https://guptagaurav.info/articles/earnings-drift)). Our test was stricter than the academic ones: it charged costs, required liquidity, compared against the industry and bought only. A gross 4-5% gap shrinks to 1-2% under those conditions, which is about the cost of trading. Passing in 2018-22 and failing in 2023-24 is what a shrinking, crowded effect looks like. Indian brokers do not publish backtests of either signal; their "order inflow" and "results surprise" notes are stock stories, not tests.

## The SME "order win" is partly a manipulation tool

The SME claims are where the gap between what people say and what can be measured is widest. **SEBI has said in writing that order-win style announcements are part of the playbook.** Its August 2024 advisory warns that some SME promoters "project an unrealistic picture of their operations through public announcements". They follow these with bonus issues or splits, then "offload their holdings at elevated prices" ([SEBI](https://investor.sebi.gov.in/advisory-investment-in-sme-segment.html)). The textbook case is **Kalahridhaan Trendz** on NSE Emerge. It announced a Rs 115 crore export order, about 60% of its revenue, from a Bangladesh buyer that did not exist; the "buyer's" email had been created in India. The stock went from 1-3 trades a day to two straight 20% upper circuits. It then fell 20% twice and was below its pre-news price within four sessions ([SEBI order via MSEI](https://www.msei.in/SX-Content/Circulars/2025/February/Circular-16749.pdf)). Anyone posting "up 40% in two days on order win" was right for two days. Anyone buying the day-1 close, as our rule does, bought near the top. The same pattern shows up in DroneAcharya, which made false announcements while pre-IPO investors sold Rs 114 crore of shares ([Inc42](https://inc42.com/buzz/sebi-cracks-down-on-droneacharya-founders-over-ipo-fraud-revenue-inflation)). It shows up in Gensol, whose "30,000 EV pre-orders" were non-binding MoUs ([Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/corporate/sebi-finds-no-ev-activity-at-gensols-pune-plant-rs-262-cr-misused-how-were-funds-misused)). And it shows up in the May 2026 order against a group that bought thin SME stocks, pushed stories on X, Telegram and WhatsApp, and sold into the buying ([Angel One](https://www.angelone.in/news/market-updates/sebi-bars-hemant-gupta-6-others-in-alleged-social-media-linked-stock-manipulation-case)).

Two more biases keep the claims alive. First, **survivorship**: people post the calls that worked, and failed SME stocks drop into restricted trading or quietly disappear. In 2026, 70 of 151 new SME listings traded below their issue price, while the mainboard IPO index was up over 10% ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/markets/news/sme-listings-sink-as-mainboard-ipo-index-delivers-over-10-annual-return-126100400367_1.html)). Second, **the loudest advisers are the worst**. In a study of 29,000 social-media stock posters, about 56% picked stocks that went on to lose about 2.3% a month, and those posters had more followers than the skilled minority. Doing the opposite of their advice earned about 1.2% a month ([SFI](https://www.sfi.ch/en/publications/n-23-30-finfluencers); [Quantpedia](https://quantpedia.com/what-can-we-extract-from-the-financial-influencers-advice/)). Even where an SME jump is genuine, it is hard to trade. Exchanges place volatile small stocks under special surveillance, with 2-5% daily limits, trade-for-trade settlement (no same-day selling) and full cash margin ([Zerodha](https://support.zerodha.com/category/trading-and-markets/alerts-and-nudges/nudges/articles/esm)). Large fixed lot sizes also make exits hard when prices fall. A backtest that assumes we can buy and sell freely at the close would overstate SME returns. Our Rs 5 crore/day floor already keeps almost all of these stocks out, which is a feature, not a gap.

Fake orders tend to share features we could screen for. The buyer is foreign or private and cannot be checked. The value is a large round number with no delivery dates. The filing comes just after a weekend. Key staff have recently resigned. The news lands near a promoter lock-in expiry or a fresh share issue. Genuine orders tend to name a government buyer, a scope and a value, and they show up as revenue in the next two to four quarters.

## Eight rules worth testing, best first

The rules below differ from ours in the ways described above. Each can be built from data we hold: NSE announcements 2018-2025 with order values, results with publish times, adjusted daily prices, Stage 2, industries and historical market value. Rows 1 and 2 are not strategies. They are exploration rows (no claim of an edge) that explain where the practitioners' money came from, and they should run first because they decide whether the rest are worth trying. Every other row is a pre-registered test on 2018-22, counted within one family for the false-discovery check. Only the rule or rules that pass get one look at the unread 2023-2025 orders. **That clean check can be spent only once**, so it should not be used on exploration.

| Rank | Rule (plain words) | Why it might work | Data we have | Promise / ease |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Measure the price move from 20 days before an order filing to the day after (exploration only) | Tests the "already priced in" story directly; Korea and US both show a pre-filing rise | Announcements + daily prices | Diagnostic, very easy |
| 2 | Rerun our order-win events against the whole market, not the industry (exploration only) | Shows whether practitioners' gains were just the sector rising | Same events | Diagnostic, very easy |
| 3 | Results: need two to three quarters in a row of rising sales and profit, with sales growth of about 40% or more, and the stock in Stage 2 | What practitioners actually screen for; older Indian research found earnings plus price trend strongest | Quarterly results + Stage 2 | High promise, easy; needs its own fresh check (2025 results, if not yet read) |
| 4 | Buy only if the reaction holds: day-0/day-1 close near the high, volume several times normal; skip weak reactions | "Let the price decide" is the most consistent practitioner rule | Daily prices + volume + filing times | Medium-high, easy |
| 5 | Wait for the stock to go sideways in a tight range for 2-6 weeks after the news, then buy the break above it | Avoids the fade (Kalahridhaan), matches "Burst & Base" | Daily prices | Medium, more settings to fix in advance |
| 6 | Order win confirmed by results: buy only when the next quarter's sales growth shows the order turning into revenue | Practitioners' own confirmation; filters fake and slow orders | Announcements joined to next results | Medium, easy join, later entry |
| 7 | Size the order against market value instead of revenue, and favour government/PSU buyers over private, foreign or related-party ones | The only factor that mattered in the US study was size against market value; fakes use unverifiable buyers | Historical market value; buyer type needs a classifier pass | Medium, the buyer field takes some work |
| 8 | Same rules with a tight stop (e.g. 8% or below the reaction-day low) and a trailing exit instead of a fixed 60 days | Matches how traders actually hold | Daily prices | Low-medium; changes what is measured and adds settings |

Two ideas are left off the table on purpose. Testing SME or sub-Rs 5 crore stocks would mostly measure manipulation episodes and fills that could not happen. Insider-buying filters need PIT (insider-trading) disclosures we do not collect. Two cautions apply to every row. Rows 3-5 change the entry day, so they must be judged against the same industry and Stage 2 peers as before, or they will just rediscover the sector rally. And row 3 cannot use 2023-24 as its check, because that period has already been read for results.

## Conclusion

Our tests and the public claims are mostly not in conflict. They measure different things. Practitioners describe either the first hours of news, often in stocks we cannot or should not trade, or multi-year sector rallies. Those stories are filtered for winners and told by people who are, on average, poor stock pickers. What our tests isolate, a tradable edge after the news is public that beats the stock's own industry, looks close to zero in every serious study we found. On results, the effect was real and is fading. The next step is not to loosen our rules toward the claims. It is to run the two cheap exploration rows first. If most of the move happens before the filing, or the "edge" disappears once the sector rally is removed, the order-win question is closed without spending the 2023-2025 check. If not, the strongest candidate is the multi-quarter, sales-led, trend-confirmed results rule, and it needs its own fresh check period.

One uncertainty remains. The X, Telegram and YouTube material where the most specific trader rules live could not be retrieved. A practitioner rule better than the ones above may exist, but nobody has published its track record.
