A field guide

Stockey, explained

A research system that tries to beat simply owning the NIFTY index — after real trading costs — and stays honest about when it can’t. It advises; a person always decides.

Goal

What it’s trying to do

Beat buy-and-hold NIFTY, after costs — and never trade on its own.

The only scorecard that matters is a plain one: did our picks beat the index over the same stretch, once trading costs are paid? Everything else is a means to that. And it is REVIEW-ONLY — the system proposes; a human approves before anything happens. It cannot place a trade by itself.: it proposes, a person approves.

The detail

No broker automation exists; the master switch defaults OFF. Every decision carries its evidence and an authority stamp (portfolio_authority = none, no broker), so nothing review-only can leak into execution.

Data

Get the prices right first

Before any clever analysis, make sure the numbers are real.

A SPLIT / BONUS — a company swaps each ₹1000 share for ten ₹100 shares (or hands out free ones). Your holding is worth the same; only the per-share price changes. turns a ₹1000 share into ₹100 overnight — it looks like a −90% crash, but nothing happened. We adjust for splits and bonuses so a real move is never confused with a bookkeeping event. We also keep tracking a name after it’s moved to a surveillance list, instead of losing it.

The detail

We derive ADJUSTED PRICE — the past price re-scaled so a split/bonus doesn’t show up as a fake jump. Returns are measured on these, so they reflect real gains and losses only. from the price steps themselves; treat a name as one continuous instrument across the EQ→BE segment change; and always compare a stock to the index over the stock’s actual holding window, never a fixed row offset. A durable audit re-checks all of this on every run.

Signal

Does our stock-picking actually work?

We measured it honestly. Mostly it works only when the whole market is already rising.

Our score has five parts — trend, base structure, volume participation, relative strength, and tradability. We checked whether a higher score really leads to a higher return. The answer: a small edge in an up-market, and roughly nothing — sometimes worse — when the market is weak. That is mostly riding the market, not skill.

The detail

Measured as RANK-IC — a score from −1 to +1 for how well a signal’s ranking of stocks matches what actually happened next. 0 means no relationship; even +0.05 across many names is real money. against forward index-excess return. In a rising tape (index above its 50-day average) the useful parts score about +0.10; in a weak tape about 0 — that gap is BETA vs ALPHA — beta is just moving with the market; alpha is skill that works regardless. An edge that only appears when the market rises is beta wearing a costume.. With only ~13 independent time-blocks the confidence bands are wide, so we don’t over-claim. Two parts were even mis-signed — a big breakout-day volume spike predicted fading, not follow-through.

Edge

So what actually works?

Two things: owning the strongest recent performers, and not losing in downturns.

A simple book — own the ~20 names with the strongest three-month momentum, rebalanced — beat the index by roughly 7–8%. And the bigger lever isn’t sharper picking: it’s not being fully invested into a crash.

The detail

The strength signal is RELATIVE STRENGTH (momentum) — how a stock’s recent return ranks against every other stock. High-rank names have been the market’s leaders; the tendency for leaders to keep leading is momentum.: a cross-sectional rank of 3-to-12-month returns. Net of costs the book returned about +7% versus the index’s −1% over ~13 months — encouraging, but it’s a single market REGIME — a stretch of market with one character (calm grind, crash, recovery). Our data holds essentially one, so results are “promising,” not “proven all-weather.”, so it’s treated as promising, not proven.

Risk

The not-losing layer

Size each position by how jumpy it is, and pull back when the market looks sick.

A more volatile stock gets a smaller slice, so no single name can hurt too much. And when the market turns unhealthy, the system cuts how much money is deployed at all — a CRASH FLOOR — a pre-committed rule that reduces how much of the account is invested when the market is weak, and restores it when the market heals. Insurance against a bad stretch, decided in advance..

The detail

Positions are volatility-targeted: risk a fixed slice of capital to a STOP / ATR — a pre-set exit price. Its distance is a few “ATRs” (Average True Range = a stock’s typical daily swing), so jumpier stocks get wider stops and therefore smaller positions. set a few ATRs away, capped at 5% a name. In testing, the crash floor cut the worst DRAWDOWN — the biggest peak-to-trough drop in the account along the way. A −20% drawdown means it fell a fifth from its high before recovering. roughly in half — it’s insurance, not extra return.

Market

Which “market” do we watch?

Not just NIFTY — because NIFTY can look healthy while most stocks are falling.

NIFTY is dominated by a handful of giant companies. BREADTH — the share of stocks trading above their own 50-day average price. It measures the market most stocks actually live in, which a few giant companies can’t mask. asks a simpler question: what share of all stocks are above their own 50-day average? It catches narrow, top-heavy markets that the headline index hides.

The detail

NIFTY’s health and breadth agree only about 2 days in 3. Using breadth as the crash-floor trigger beat using NIFTY — same-or-better drawdown protection with more return. But for timing which stocks to pick, NIFTY stayed the better guide. Same word, two different jobs.

Dial

Letting the data set the caution level

You don’t hand-pick how cautious to be. You pick a goal; the data picks the setting.

The goal we committed to: grow the account, but never risk ruin. The system tries many caution settings and reports the one that grows the most while staying under a wide worst-loss cap. You choose the objective once; you never twist a knob.

The detail

Formally: maximise COMPOUNDED GROWTH — the account’s actual multiplying-up over time (gains build on gains), not the average of separate periods. It’s what you care about because it’s the money you end with. subject to a RUIN-GUARD — a wide, pre-set maximum-loss line (default −20%). Settings that would breach it are thrown out first; among the survivors, the data keeps the one that grows most. max-drawdown floor of −20%. On today’s data that picks a light touch — stay mostly invested, trim modestly when breadth is weak. As tougher periods appear, the same rule tightens on its own.

Product

The daily advisory

Each day: today’s strongest names, how much to put in each, and where the stop goes — for review.

The system publishes a daily list: the strength-ranked picks, sized by the risk layer and the current market health, each with a stop and a cost estimate. A person reviews and decides. Nothing executes on its own.

The detail

Written to a review-only table with an explicit no-broker stamp on every row, refreshed by a small scheduled chain each evening after the close: clean prices → breadth → the momentum book → the floor setting → the advisory.

The detail — two paths, side by side

Two systems run in parallel, and they connect differently:

The elaborate funnel

A five-part technical score → “confirmed entry” → … and stops there. It makes almost no buys — the last step (turning a good entry into an actual BUY) was never built, and we MEASURED WEAK — a high technical score only led to higher returns when the whole market was already rising; in a weak tape it was ~0 or backwards. That’s riding the market, not skill. anyway. This is the empty “Recommendations” screen.

The working path

Breadth sizes the book → relative strength picks the names → the advisory & the paper book produce the daily decision and manage every exit. This is the Advisory tab — the one actually earning its keep.

So today the fancy technical score is measured, flagged weak, and mostly on the sidelines; the real decisions come from regime-sizing + relative strength.

Discipline

How we keep ourselves honest

We assume we’ll fool ourselves, and design against it.

Thirteen months isn’t much history, so we resist tuning knobs to fit it. New ideas ship switched off and only turn on once more evidence backs them. Every failure or fallback is made visible rather than quietly swallowed.

The detail

Concretely: ~13 independent time-blocks cap what we can claim; changes must survive WALK-FORWARD — test a rule on one stretch, then check it still holds on the next, unseen stretch. A rule that only works on the data it was built from doesn’t pass. testing and false-discovery control; candidate fixes live behind default-off switches; and everything is POINT-IN-TIME — every decision uses only what was actually known that day. No peeking at the future, which is the easiest way to build a backtest that lies. — no peeking at the future.
